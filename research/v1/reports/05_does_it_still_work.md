## Does it still work

**Bottom line:**
*   Post Earnings Announcement Drift and Information Rebalancing have the strongest evidence for surviving costs in recent years.
*   Net returns after costs range from 0.37% to 0.92% per month for robust strategies, but many older signals fail.
*   Holding periods vary from 21 days for rebalancing to 120 days for earnings drift, with shorter periods generally surviving costs better.

### The methods with the best evidence

The most reliable patterns link specific news events to price movements over weeks. Post Earnings Announcement Drift remains a persistent anomaly where stocks continue to move in the direction of an earnings surprise for months. One study found a long extreme profit and short extreme loss hedge portfolio earned 10.21% abnormal return over 120 days, which is robust to transaction costs up to 100 basis points per trade [P3277]. This effect is stronger when information uncertainty is high or when local search bias is present [P3, P3016]. However, the magnitude has declined in recent years [P8].

Information Rebalancing offers a precise timing mechanism. Returns concentrate in the first month after a signal is released, such as an earnings release or 10-K filing. A strategy triggered on the exact date of information release generated 6.40% annualized abnormal return during the February to April window from 2005 to 2019 [P3512]. This suggests that acting immediately on news detection is critical.

Fundamental signals like Accruals and Asset Growth also show promise. Accruals strategies sort stocks by the difference between earnings and cash flow. One study reported a gross excess return of 0.42% per month, with net returns varying by market cap [P3544]. Another found mean annual abnormal returns between 7.5% and 8.9% from 1965 to 2002 [P3776]. Asset Growth strategies, which buy slow growers and sell fast growers, show gross excess returns of 0.84% per month [P3544]. These methods rely on annual data but can be updated as filings arrive.

### Where it is weak or disagrees

Many historical patterns fail when tested against modern costs and publication effects. A broad replication of 452 anomalies found that 65% fail the statistical hurdle with NYSE breakpoints, and 82% fail stricter tests [P3629]. Average anomaly expected return is estimated at 8 basis points per month net of costs and post publication effects, while academic implementations show negative returns [P3511]. Publication significantly erodes returns, with portfolio returns 58% lower post publication compared to the original sample [P3531]. A text based version of earnings drift, built from the language of earnings calls, is reported to be larger than classic drift and still considerable in recent years when classic drift is close to zero; it reports no strategy return and was read from the abstract and citing papers only, and a citing paper notes that drift has largely disappeared at multi day horizons [P31].

Trading costs are a major barrier for retail traders. Round trip transaction costs average over 50 basis points per trade, reducing realized spreads by more than 1% for every 1% of monthly turnover [P3544]. Implementation costs for momentum strategies range from 2.2% to 8.3% per year, eliminating most profits [P3766]. Some studies report no strategy return after costs in specific markets. For example, one paper reports no strategy return after costs in the UK from 1989 to 2004, where the hedge portfolio generates losses when equally weighted after conservative transaction costs [P3773]. Another study states over 30 strategies delivered significant returns before costs in Emerging Europe, yet only a handful remained profitable after costs and constraints [P3768].

Shorting constraints often drive anomaly profits. Average long short portfolio return is 0.14% per month before short sale costs, but -0.01% after borrow fees [P3532]. Some papers report no strategy return but provide important benchmarks. One paper reports no strategy return but benchmarks trading costs, finding annualized costs for long/short portfolios range from 17 to 41 basis points, falling to 9 to 21 basis points excluding microcaps when using closing auctions [P3770]. Another finds small traders trade seasonal random walk surprises while large traders trade analyst based surprises, noting drift attenuates when respective groups react thoroughly [P10].

Momentum strategies face significant crash risks. A static winners minus losers strategy had an annualized alpha of 22.3% from 1927 to 2013 but suffered severe crashes in panic states [P6211]. Momentum has a monthly return of 1.44% in the full sample but is insignificant in the second sub period from 1994 to 2011 [P3769].

### What this means for a trader

Traders must prioritize liquidity and timing to capture these returns. Strategies with turnover below 50% survive costs using buy and hold spread rules [P3544]. Retail traders should avoid high turnover strategies greater than 50% monthly as costs wipe out profits [P3766]. Short signals should be blocked on high cost stocks because borrow fees often negate profits [P3532].

Focus on the immediate post release window for news. Information rebalancing generated 6.40% annualized abnormal return during the February to April window [P3512]. This implies that waiting too long after a news event reduces the edge. Also, be aware that most historical patterns fail strict statistical tests in recent years [P3629]. A trader should treat historical returns as upper bounds rather than guarantees.

### What this means for the app

The app must filter for liquidity to prevent cost erosion. One study validates a method to estimate bid ask spreads from OHLC data, showing 0.74 correlation with TAQ benchmarks [P6810]. The app should integrate this estimator as a cost filter to prevent trading unprofitable small caps. It should also include a decay factor reducing expected returns based on publication age [P3531].

The app should flag earnings on high distraction days or Fridays for delayed entry to capture drift [P82, P81]. It must trigger signals immediately upon news or filing detection with short holding periods of 1 to 4 weeks [P3512]. Short signals should be blocked on high cost stocks [P3532]. The app should link industry leaders to followers with a delay warning [P1184]. Finally, the app should warn users that shorting is often inaccessible for retail due to fees [P5854].
