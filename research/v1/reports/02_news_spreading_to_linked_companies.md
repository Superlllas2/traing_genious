## News spreading to linked companies

**Bottom line:**
*   Connected peer momentum and customer supplier links offer the strongest evidence for returns between 0.85% and 1.45% per month over one month holding periods.
*   Distress and disaster spillovers provide clear negative signals for shorting customers of hit suppliers, with returns around -2.37% over 51 days.
*   Many effects decay in recent years or vanish after transaction costs, so holding periods should range from one week to three months depending on the signal type.

### The methods with the best evidence

The most robust method family is Connected Peer Momentum. This approach trades on the idea that firms sharing analysts, technology, or geography move together. Research shows shared analyst coverage generates a monthly five factor alpha of 1.19% for value weighted portfolios in the US from 1984 to 2015 [P6243]. This effect persists in the 2000 to 2015 period but is weaker. Another study reports a 1.29% monthly return for trading technological peers based on patent similarity [P1914]. Geographic peer returns yield a 60 basis point monthly alpha over 1990 to 2012, strongest at one month lags [P1049]. The holding period is typically one month. Turnover is high at 313% per month, implying a break even cost of 42.5 basis points.

Customer and Supplier Momentum is the second strong family. News flows from customers to suppliers and vice versa. One study finds customer momentum yields 1.45% monthly abnormal returns from 1981 to 2004 [P83]. Supplier prices underreact by 40% initially and drift over 12 months. Another report finds Chinese customer returns predict US supplier returns with 2.179% monthly abnormal returns from 2009 to 2015 [P1185]. However, some evidence suggests this effect is subsumed by broader momentum factors [P6243].

Distress and Litigation Spillovers offer the clearest short signals. Negative shocks spread rapidly. When a supplier is hit by a disaster, customer firms drop 1.13% cumulative average abnormal return over 51 days, rising to -2.37% for specific inputs [P326]. Suppliers lose 19% of market cap when a partner files for bankruptcy with less than 40% reorganization probability [P1839]. Peers lose -0.33% over 9 days before a lawsuit filing and -4.21% after if settled [P1831]. These events require immediate action, often within days.

Earnings Drift remains relevant. Late announcers often overreact to early peer news. One study finds a 1.16% average hedge return over the 3-day window around a late announcer's own earnings from 1973 to 2005 [P1755]. Post Earnings Announcement Drift shows 4.2% abnormal returns over 60 days [P5]. Friday earnings announcements have a 15% lower immediate price response but 70% higher delayed drift, earning 3.84% per month abnormal return [P81].

### Where it is weak or disagrees

Several methods work only in small stocks or before costs. Conglomerate strategies generate 95 basis points monthly returns but require shorting and paid segment data [P1568]. The most delayed firms command a return premium of more than 12% per year, though this involves small, illiquid stocks [P5792]. Many studies end before 2015, and costs are rarely deducted. For example, P6243 notes the analyst effect is weaker in recent years. P1179 finds supplier effects vanish for large stocks and recent periods.

There are conflicting results on the source of profits. P5788 finds positive cross autocovariances account for 67% of contrarian profits, while P6182 finds overreaction to firm specific news accounts for 105% of profits [P5788, P6182]. P2163 states transaction costs likely overwhelm potential profits for volume based strategies [P2163].

Some papers report no strategy return despite finding statistical links. P5826 finds returns on high analyst portfolios lead low analyst portfolios, but specific numbers are not reported [P5826]. P117 provides regression coefficients of 0.2304 for intra industry big to small lead versus 0.0294 inter industry over 1963 to 2001 [P117]. The effect comes mostly after bad news: small firms' response to big firm returns is 0.2841 after negative weeks and 0.1283 after positive weeks, and it sits in small stocks at a weekly horizon [P117]. P1759 supports overreaction hypotheses but exact return figures are not reported [P1759]. P1764 finds overreaction in competitive industries, but no specific return numbers are provided [P1764]. P1824 finds semi annual reporters overreact to peers, but exact returns are not provided [P1824]. P1746 finds earnings announcements by one firm significantly impact the stock prices of non announcing industry peers, but reports no strategy return [P1746].

Supply chain attention studies show mixed results. P1660 finds low co search attention increases predictability, but specific returns are not reported [P1660]. P1187 finds customer industries lead supplier industries across 22 countries, but numbers are not reported [P1187]. P1189 finds retail attention eliminates predictability, but returns are not reported [P1189]. P1661 finds corruption scandals hurt bystander buyers, but specific return percentages are not reported [P1661]. P1763 finds significant abnormal returns for dividend changes, but no number is provided in the abstract [P1763].

Other structural links show no clear return. P5984 finds returns on high institutional ownership portfolios lead low ownership portfolios, with the effect persisting after controlling for size, but reports no strategy return [P5984]. P6065 finds non bankrupt strategic alliance partners experience a negative stock price reaction, with profit margins decreasing over the subsequent two years, but reports no strategy return [P6065]. P1059 finds regional economic activity forecasts positively predict cross sectional stock returns, but reports no strategy return [P1059]. P1847 finds extracted dynamic networks provide additional value in predicting firm equity value, but reports no strategy return [P1847]. P1057 finds firms with low R&D growth but high exposure to industry R&D spillovers experience positive abnormal returns, but reports no strategy return [P1057]. P1758 confirms the overreaction anomaly exists with SIC classification but disappears in recent years with GICS, and reports no strategy return [P1758].

### What this means for a trader

You should focus on specific events rather than general lead lag patterns. Use shared analyst and customer links for one month signals. Flag specific input suppliers for disaster news to short immediately. Use retail attention filters to suppress signals when attention is high. Short litigation peers immediately but expect rapid reaction. Be aware that many effects decay in recent years. Transaction costs are a major hurdle for high turnover strategies like analyst links.

### What this means for the app

The app should prioritize 'shared analyst' and 'customer' links for 1-month signals. It must flag 'specific input' suppliers for disaster news. Use 'retail attention' filters to suppress signals when attention is high. Short litigation peers immediately. Parse analyst report text for sentiment overrides over 2 days. Map industry networks to predict merger waves over 1 to 3 years. Monitor regional exposure for economic shocks.
