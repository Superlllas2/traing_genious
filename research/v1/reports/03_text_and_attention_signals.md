## Text and attention signals

**Bottom line:**
*   The strongest evidence supports large language model news sentiment and filing tone changes for short term drift.
*   After transaction costs, daily returns range from 10 to 30 basis points for news signals and 4 percent over 60 days for earnings surprises.
*   Holding periods are typically one day for news, two weeks for search spikes, and two months for earnings announcements.

### The methods with the best evidence

The most robust strategies rely on modern language models to read news headlines and corporate filings. Large language models like GPT-4 can score overnight news to predict daily returns. One study found an equal weighted strategy using GPT-4 scores yielded a mean daily return of 0.122 percent with a Sharpe ratio of 2.13 after five basis points of cost [P147]. Another model using OPT generated a 355 percent gain between August 2021 and July 2023 with a Sharpe ratio of 3.05 after 10 basis points of cost [P2452]. A third approach using SESTM strategies netted 11.23 basis points per day with a Sharpe ratio of 2.30 after 10 basis points of costs [P2129]. These signals work best when held for one day because the information incorporates quickly.

Filing text analysis offers a medium term alternative. Changes in the wording of 10-K and 10-Q filings predict drift over six to 18 months, with no jump on the filing day. Buying firms whose filings did not change and shorting those whose filings changed earned 34 to 58 basis points per month, value weighted and before costs; changes in the Risk Factors section alone reached up to 188 basis points per month, equally weighted [P6261]. Another study using the Fin Neg dictionary found a regression coefficient of -19.538 for four day excess returns when high negative word proportions were present [P6294]. This suggests that reading the specific language in regulatory documents provides a durable signal compared to general news.

Attention spikes measured by search volume predict short term momentum followed by reversal. Abnormal search volume increases predicted 18.7 basis points in the first week and 14.9 basis points in the second week [P254]. However, this effect reverses by week four. Earnings announcements also drive predictable drift. High versus low standardized unexpected earnings deciles yielded 4.2 percent abnormal return over 60 days [P5]. This effect was stronger for small firms at 5.3 percent. Finally, stocks with no media coverage outperformed high coverage stocks by 0.23 percent per month [P6298].

### Where it is weak or disagrees

Many strategies fail when transaction costs are applied or when tested on recent data. Dictionary based sentiment often loses profitability after costs. One long short strategy showed an alpha of 10.31 basis points per day before costs but dropped to -4.37 percent after 10 basis points of costs [P6039]. Another BERT based portfolio saw daily raw returns of 0.242 percent drop to 0.012 percent after 0.23 percent transaction costs [P2050]. This indicates that retail traders with higher costs may not capture these gains.

Several studies report no strategy return despite finding statistical significance. Some papers found daily news predicts returns for one to two days and weekly news for one quarter, but today's news has no predictive power for tomorrow [P2542]. Others found correlation coefficients between news and volume greater than 0.4, yet today's news has no predictive power for tomorrow [P2563]. Search volume increases predict significant decreases in index returns in some contexts [P2734]. In the Indian market, high search volumes predict positive significant returns in the 4th and 5th weeks [P2753]. Local investor attention spikes predict future price reversals in US S&P 500 stocks [P2745]. Attention predictability is short lived and asymmetric, with negative attention predicting returns better than optimism [P2738].

Social media signals often predict volatility rather than direction. Forum sentiment correlates with volatility but not individual stock returns [P6299]. Tweet content predicts FOMC meeting returns [P6387]. Twitter sentiment predicts volume and returns, stronger in small or emerging markets [P1997]. Some sentiment factors are not priced in the cross section [P1994]. Positive news sentiment predicts higher returns, strongest in low attention stocks, but reports no strategy return [P2544]. High pollution news beta stocks earn higher returns only when media sentiment is positive [P2008]. Abnormal positive tone predicts positive returns in initial and 60-day windows [P2672].

Other text signals show weak or mixed results. Activity and Realism indicators predict price increases with R squared less than 4 percent [P2011]. Credibility attenuates post call drift [P2689]. Analyst praise predicts abnormal returns over the following quarter [P2680]. V shaped returns occur around forced CEO turnovers [P851]. A composite investor attention index predicts market risk premium, though individual proxies do not [P2733]. Attention based buying yields negative alphas [P5774].

### What this means for a trader

You should focus on signals that survive transaction costs. News sentiment works best if you trade daily and keep costs under 10 basis points. Earnings drift allows for longer holding periods of 60 days which reduces turnover costs. Be cautious with search volume signals because they reverse after two weeks. Small stocks often show stronger effects but have higher trading costs. Avoid strategies that rely on social media comments as they often predict volatility rather than price direction.

### What this means for the app

The app should prioritize LLM scoring of news headlines for daily signals. It must extract risk factor changes from 10-K filings for medium term holds. Search volume spikes should trigger a warning to exit positions after two weeks to avoid reversal. The system should warn users when transaction costs exceed 10 basis points as profitability drops sharply. Social media sentiment should be used to flag volatility risks rather than directional trades.
