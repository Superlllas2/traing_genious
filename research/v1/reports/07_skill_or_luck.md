## Skill or luck

**Bottom line:**
*   Mutual fund momentum and avoiding herding show the strongest evidence for persistent returns.
*   Most academic anomalies earn near zero returns after costs and publication.
*   Holding periods range from two weeks for attention signals to one year for fund selection.

### The methods with the best evidence

The most reliable way to find skill is to look at mutual fund performance over long periods. The best supported method is mutual fund momentum. You rank all mutual funds by their total return over the previous 12 months. You then hold the top decile against the bottom decile for 252 days or one year. This strategy shows an annualized spread of approximately 8 percent between winners and losers. This return is net of operating expenses and transaction costs. The evidence is strong and comes from full text analysis of P3681. This method is backed by 3 method cards.

Another strong method involves avoiding funds that follow the crowd. You calculate a fund herding score. If the score is in the top decile, the fund has the highest tendency to follow past institutional trades. You hold this position for 63 days or one quarter. These funds underperform antiherding funds by 19 basis points per month. This equals 2.28 percent per year. The results hold for both net and gross returns. This implies the gap exists before and after fees. The evidence is strong based on P5067. This method is backed by 2 method cards.

For shorter timeframes, search volume and attention offer signals. You look for abnormal search volume increases. This is the current week log search volume minus the 8 week median. You hold for about 10 days or approximately 2 weeks. This predicts abnormal returns of 18.7 basis points in week 1 and 14.9 basis points in week 2. The evidence is strong based on P254. This method is backed by 4 method cards.

However, many academic patterns do not survive. Anomaly portfolio construction shows this clearly. You use 120 signals to build long short portfolios. The holding period varies by anomaly but is typically monthly. Post publication and post 2005, the return is -0.03 percent per month. This means the strategy loses money after costs. The evidence is strong based on P3511. This method is backed by 7 method cards.

Long run reversal is another method to consider. You look at stock returns between months t-36 and t-13. You hold for about 21 days or 1 month. The long short portfolio earns 0.10 percent a month with a t stat of 3.29. However, returns are before trading costs. The evidence is strong based on P5772 and P615. This method is backed by 8 method cards.

### Where it is weak or disagrees

Many methods fail when tested rigorously. Anomaly decay is a major weakness. Portfolio returns are 58 percent lower post publication across 97 predictors. In sample mean return is 58.2 basis points per month. Post 2005, cost optimized net return is 13 basis points per month. Equal weighted is -3 basis points. This suggests most published signals are luck or decay quickly. P3531 and P3511 support this.

Statistical validation often fails in real markets. A strategy with an annualized Sharpe ratio of 2.5 over 5 years is statistically insignificant if it resulted from 88 independent trials. It is only significant if it resulted from only 46 trials. P5341 shows this. In synthetic markets, the MinervaScore separates true signals from lucky backtests with an AUROC of 0.989. But in a pre registered real market test, the score showed no significant forward relationship. P5373 reports this disagreement.

Data quality is also a problem. Many studies end before 2010. P5772 ends in 1980. P413 data is 1991 to 1996. P5472 is US 1991 to 1996. This limits applicability to modern markets. Costs are often not reported. P1424 does not report net returns after costs. P5772 does not calculate net costs. P3531 does not calculate net costs for average strategy.

Individual investors generally struggle to distinguish skill from luck. The average investor underperforms by 1.5 percent per year. Active traders underperform by 6.5 percent annually. P5472 finds this. Stocks bought underperform stocks sold by 23 basis points per month over 12 months. P5472 shows this. This suggests frequent trading destroys wealth.

### What this means for a trader

You must demand long track records to prove skill. A fund needs 10 years of data to distinguish skill from luck. P5192 suggests this. You should avoid high turnover winners. Top funds with high turnover underperform low turnover funds by 1.6 percent to 1.9 percent per year. P5483 shows this. You should also check for herding. Herding funds underperform antiherding funds. P5067 confirms this.

Do not trust published anomalies blindly. Most decay significantly. P3531 finds a 58 percent decay post publication. You should prioritize low turnover signals. P3511 shows cost optimized returns are near zero post 2005. You should reduce your own turnover. Frequent trading destroys wealth. P5472 and P413 support this. You should also avoid buying stocks with massive single day gains. P129 finds stocks with high maximum daily returns underperform.

### What this means for the app

The app must implement a skill versus luck filter. Use bootstrapping to test fund performance. P5041 suggests this. The app should flag high turnover after gains. P5483 shows this is a negative signal. The app must implement a Deflated Sharpe Ratio calculator. This penalizes strategies found after extensive searching. P5341 and P5612 support this.

The app should track trial counts. Testing only 10 alternative strategy configurations on random data yields an in sample Sharpe ratio of 1.57. P5612 demonstrates this. The app should use False Discovery Rate control when ranking news triggers. P6688 establishes this. The app should implement a replication ratio warning for complex strategies. P5351 suggests simple models with long data histories are less likely to be luck.

The app should integrate a Retail Attention metric. This flags insider trades. P5477 shows insider sales during high attention months are followed by lower returns. The app should warn users that short term news signals often decay. P5351 notes this. Finally, the app should warn users that past winners are often lucky. P5041 finds fewer than 16 percent of funds have true alpha.
