## From signal to trade
**Bottom line:** Size factors and earnings drift show the strongest evidence for returns after costs. Hold positions for one month to one year to capture these effects. High turnover strategies fail once execution costs are included.

### The methods with the best evidence
The strongest evidence comes from composite factor forecasts and volatility management. Size factors sort stocks by market capitalization. Small stocks outperform large stocks over one year. P3544 reports a net excess return of 0.28% per month after costs of 0.04% per month. This strategy survives costs because turnover is low. P6807 supports this with a 2.43% per month spread for 15-characteristic forecasts, though power weakens post-2004.

Volatility management offers another robust path. P4152 reports a 4.86% annualized alpha for a volatility managed Market Portfolio using US data from 1926 to 2015. This survives high volatility costs of 10bps plus 4bps. P3834 finds a 0.73% per month CAPM alpha for US equities over a similar long period. Traders should reduce size when market volatility spikes.

Post Earnings Announcement Drift is the most actionable signal for an event driven app. P5 finds a long short strategy yields 4.2% abnormal return over 60 days. P81 reports 3.84% per month abnormal return for Friday announcements. P82 shows 1.64% per month alpha on high distraction days. The entry trigger is an earnings surprise. The hold period is 60 days. P48 confirms individual investor trading does not explain this drift, meaning the signal is not just retail noise.

Technical stop loss rules show mixed but useful evidence. P5772 reports a 24.6% return difference between loser and winner portfolios over 3 years using a long run reversal strategy. This yields 0.10% a month with a t stat of 3.29. For exit rules, P4691 finds a 15% trailing stop loss achieved 74% cumulative return on Swedish large caps. A 5% stop limit resulted in -8.1% returns. This suggests tight stops hurt performance.

### Where it is weak or disagrees
Several methods fail to produce strategy returns or rely on old data. Some papers report statistical findings without strategy returns. P5774 reports no strategy return and finds alphas for attention based buying are negative or not significantly different from zero. P117 finds regression coefficients of 0.2304 for intra industry lag but reports no strategy return. The effect comes mostly after bad news: small firms' response to big firm returns is 0.2841 after negative weeks and 0.1283 after positive weeks, and it sits in small stocks at a weekly horizon [P117]. P5981 reports no strategy return and finds price elasticity to net asset value is 0.50 normally but ~1.0 during front page news. P4269 examines tax incentives but reports no strategy return, finding the hazard rate for stock sale drops from fifteen percent to less than five percent per month after six months. P4544 simulates Kelly strategies but reports no strategy return, finding Full Kelly shows great superiority over longer horizons but exhibits very risky short term performance. P4690 claims daily stop loss rules significantly outperform buy and hold but only an abstract is available with no specific return numbers.

Costs are a major limiting factor often ignored in backtests. P5990 finds for exchange listed stocks the average total round trip cost for the smallest market cap quintile is 3.81 percent, against 0.57 percent for the largest quintile. Momentum traders averaged 2.04 percent costs. Some reported returns are before costs: the long run reversal returns [P5772], and momentum returns, where costs are not explicitly deducted [P5783]. Retail flow reversal signals need granular order flow data that retail traders typically lack [P5972]. P4260 finds individual investor portfolios suffered an annual performance penalty of 3.8 percentage points. Virtually all individual trading losses are traced to aggressive orders.

Disagreement exists on stop loss tightness. P4691 suggests 10-15% is optimal for large caps. P4694 notes fixed percentage rules improve risk adjusted returns but may reduce absolute returns in bull markets. P4698 proposes a framework for stop loss analysis but reports no numerical findings. In one model, fixed stops perform close to optimal stopping rules [P4702], while on Australian stocks (ASX200, 2000 to 2007) adding initial stops to a positive expectation trend strategy likely degraded returns [P4696]. Evidence is weak where only abstracts are available like P4690 and P4698.

### Numbers worth remembering
Specific numbers from the notes highlight the scale of effects and costs. P5990 states the average total round trip cost for the smallest market cap quintile is 3.81 percent. P4859 finds an average US investor needed 24 stocks to reduce diversifiable risk by 90%. During the 2008-2011 Global Financial Crisis this spiked to 63-72 stocks. P5777 finds IPOs average 5% annual return with a median 5-year raw return of -39%. P4152 reports 4.86% annualized alpha for volatility managed Market Portfolio. P5 finds approximately 4.2% abnormal return over 60 days for earnings drift. P4542 compares Kelly strategies on EuroStoxx50 and finds Rolling Kelly achieved a CAGR of 15.01%. P4269 finds the expected holding period for a stock in a taxable account is just over six years.

### What this means for a trader
Traders must account for the 3.8% annual penalty from aggressive trading. Holding 30-50 stocks is generally sufficient for diversification under normal conditions. Position sizing should use fractional Kelly to avoid ruin rather than full Kelly. Stop losses should be set at 10-15% to avoid noise. Strategies that turn over more than 50% of the portfolio monthly are likely to see costs wipe out profits; low turnover signals or buy and hold rules hold up better [P3544]. Watch your own selling: investors realized 20.1% of gains but only 12.6% of losses (1987 to 1990), and the winners they sold beat the losers they held by 3.41% over the next year [P4363]. Active trading destroys wealth due to costs if turnover is high. Traders should validate stops before using them on positive expectation strategies. Small caps carry high costs up to 3.81% round trip. Investors defer gains and sell losses in December creating price pressure.

### What this means for the app
The app should integrate cost estimators before suggesting trades. It should warn when a strategy turns over more than 50% of the portfolio monthly [P3544], and compare the estimated round trip cost for the stock's size, from 0.57 percent for the largest stocks to 3.81 percent for the smallest [P5990], with the expected edge. It should flag stocks with losses in December for potential selling pressure. The app should warn against fixed stops on positive expectation strategies. Position sizing must account for volatility and liquidity. The app should link leader news to followers with delay warnings for industry drift. It should flag earnings beats or misses with 60-day drift storylines. The app should implement volatility based position sizing to manage risk. It should include a diversification health metric warning of concentration risk during crises. Most evidence is pre-2000 so the app should warn on recent decay in composite factors.
